Dietary Silicate: A Biogenic Strategy to Enhance Growth, Gut Health, and Nutrient Utilization in Finishing Pigs through Low-Carbon Diets
This project investigates the effects of dietary silicate supplementation on
growth performance and gut microbiome composition in growing-finishing pigs under different

dietary protein levels. Fecal samples were collected from pigs fed high-protein or low-protein

(LP; 2% reduced crude protein) diets with or without 0.1% silicate over a 10-week feeding trial.

Microbial community structure was analyzed using sequencing-based approaches to

assess alpha and beta diversity, as well as taxonomic composition. While no significant

differences were observed in diversity indices, silicate supplementation altered microbial

composition, increasing the relative abundance of beneficial taxa such as Lactobacillus and

Lactobacillaceae. These changes were associated with improved growth performance,

enhanced nutrient digestibility, and reduced fecal emissions of ammonia, hydrogen sulfide, and

carbon dioxide.

This dataset provides insight into diet-induced modulation of the swine gut microbiome

and its relationship with nutrient utilization and environmental emissions, supporting low-carbon

feeding strategies in pig production.
